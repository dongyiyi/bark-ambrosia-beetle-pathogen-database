# Evidence and validation

The database is designed to distinguish a reported organism–beetle association from stronger evidence that the organism actively infects or causes disease in the beetle host.

## Evidence principle

The presence of an organism in, on, or near a beetle does not by itself establish pathogenicity. Depending on the source, a record may represent detection, isolation, association, parasitism, infection, or an experimentally tested pathogenic interaction.

## Validation fields

Where reported by the source, the database records validation such as:

- **infection tests** — evidence that the organism can establish infection in the focal host;
- **pathogenicity tests** — evidence that exposure or infection produces disease or mortality relative to an appropriate comparison;
- **virulence tests** — quantitative comparison of disease severity, mortality, dose response, survival, or related outcomes;
- **transmission tests** — evidence concerning movement of the pathogen between individuals or hosts.

The terminology follows the source literature as closely as practical and is standardized only enough to support comparison.

## What validation does not mean

A record with a validation field is not automatically evidence of natural prevalence, field importance, host specificity, or population-level regulation. Experimental exposure using commercial strains, culture-collection isolates, soil isolates, or isolates from other insects should be interpreted differently from naturally observed infection in the focal beetle.

## Dataset-level interpretation

The database can support analyses of how frequently different types of evidence have been reported and where validation gaps occur. It should not be used to infer prevalence or absence without study-specific sampling denominators and negative observations.
